    The problem

    Rent in Kazakhstan's cities eats most of a paycheque

    70–76%

    of a median net salary goes on a one-room flat in Astana and Almaty. Eurostat calls anything above 40% a housing cost overburden.

    CMN.KZ, from krisha.kz and BNS data, Aug 2026
    240,000 ₸

    median rent for a one-room flat in Astana (220,000 ₸ in Almaty). Rents rose 13.1% in 2025 alone.

    krisha.kz, 18 Aug 2026; BNS
    650,000+

    Kazakhstanis are on the state housing waiting list, and it moves only as fast as new construction.

    Committee for Construction and Housing, May 2025

    People without an official salary or credit history can't get a mortgage. They rent without a contract, pay cash with no receipt, and can lose their home and deposit overnight. Saving for a down payment while rent takes more than half your income is close to impossible.

    Our own research

    We read 17 public lease templates. 16 leave the tenant exposed

    When someone searches for a flat rental agreement, they download one of the first templates they find. Those are the ones we checked, clause by clause.

    16 of 17

    templates contain at least one clause against the tenant. In 13 the risk is serious: eviction without proper notice, harsh penalties, emergency repairs at the tenant's cost, a deposit that isn't returned. The one exception is the government form for municipal housing.

    10 of 17

    let the landlord end the lease and evict with no notice or less than 30 days: 2, 5 or 10 days, or "immediately". One law firm's template lets the landlord cut off electricity, water and heating and seal the flat after 10 banking days of arrears.

    2 of 17

    require proof of payment, and both are government forms. Five allow or prescribe cash without a receipt. Of 7 templates with a deposit, only 3 say when it must be returned.

    AffordBase desk study, October 2026. We checked 76 links found through searches in Russian and Kazakh; 17 unique templates (plus 6 translations) were freely available in full. Sources include krisha.kz (two versions), Otbasy Bank, the Kazakhstan Housing Company, government forms, legal websites and law firms. Each text was read against an 18-point checklist: 8 risks and 10 protections.

    How we know the lease check works

    We hand-labelled 30 risky clauses in the first 13 templates. The first version of our rules caught only 2 of them. We rebuilt the rules: on the same set the check now finds 29 of 30 with no false alarms, and it raises none on our own tenant-friendly lease in all three languages.

    Because the rules were tuned on those clauses, we then tested them honestly: on 4 new templates the rules had never seen, the check found 8 of 11 risky clauses, again with no false alarms. It missed a deposit kept "by notice", a fine written in tenge, and emergency repairs at the tenant's cost; those rules are next on our list. The sample is still small, so we keep measuring accuracy on new leases.

    How it works

    Three steps from renting to owning

    1. 1

      Match

      The matcher finds people with compatible schedules, habits and budgets. Instead of a credit history, we use a reliability index built from utility payments and income records.

      Try the matcher →
    2. 2

      Rent safely

      A fair co-lease, a check for unfair clauses, a tamper-evident photo record of the flat, and a mutual-aid fund that pays up to three months' rent if you lose income.

      Check a lease →
    3. 3

      Own, metre by metre

      Homes are built by a co-op with social investors. Each payment buys square metres, rent is charged only on the metres you don't own yet, and it shrinks every month.

          Who pays
          
      
          Lease checks and matching are free for renters, who already spend most of their income on rent. Landlords pay when they get a vetted co-renting group with a reliability index and fund cover. Later: a management fee on rent-to-own pools, and analytics for city housing programmes. Grants fund the pilot.
